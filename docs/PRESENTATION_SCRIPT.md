# Hoos Cooking presentation script

## Opening

Hoos' Cooking began with a simple question: when the price or availability of nutritious food changes in one community, how quickly can local decision-makers see it and respond? We built a country-owned system that turns locally collected food-price observations into reviewed evidence for healthier food decisions.

## Macro context

FAOSTAT gives us an essential national and global picture of healthy-diet affordability. But national annual estimates are not designed to show which local market is changing, which nutritious food is becoming harder to afford, or how conditions differ across cities in the same country. Those are questions communities need to answer locally.

## Micro reality

Families buy food in local markets shaped by seasonality, transport, weather, availability, and culture. A healthy food choice in Bogotá may not be available or affordable in Montería. Local context and local ownership therefore need to be part of the data system.

## Collection to decision workflow

A vendor or enumerator can use a digital form or a printable paper form. A paper form can be photographed or scanned. The collection pipeline extracts fields and a human reviewer confirms or corrects them before approval. With regular, governed reporting, approved records can create a more timely, country-owned evidence base that can be connected to the dashboard alongside historical validation data.

## Dashboard evidence

The Colombia pilot compares Bogotá, Medellín, and Montería across four sampled dates. The three-market view makes local variation visible. It can show a lower-cost candidate, then explain its nutrition role and tradeoffs without making an automatic recommendation. A food hub can enter a planning quantity to translate the selected per-kilogram difference into a potential gross price difference; it is clearly labeled as price-only and still requires human review.

## One-minute demo sequence

1. Show the collection and review pipeline: digital or printable-paper collection, extraction, then human review.
2. Show the Colombia locality and date controls changing the selected price action and nutrition comparison.
3. Enter 100 market kg in the procurement scenario. Explain that it scales the observed price difference for planning, not that it guarantees a completed procurement saving.
4. Point to the guardrail: the figure excludes availability, yields, transport, contracts, waste, and dietary planning.

## Close

The economic value is reduced decision latency: less time turning scattered reports into usable records and better information for directing limited procurement and assistance resources. Our principle is simple: standardize the data structure, not the diet. Hoos' Cooking connects macro measurement with local ownership and a practical micro-level path to action.
