# Three-market validation: Bogotá, Medellín, and Montería

## What changed

The original two-market pilot compares a 10-food fresh-food reference basket
between Bogotá and Medellín. To test whether the monitor can scale without
pretending that every food is equally available everywhere, we added Montería
for the same four 2025 SIPSA collection dates.

## Directly comparable shared basket

The three-market comparison uses eight foods that are present in all three
markets on every sampled date:

- Ahuyama
- Banano
- Cebolla cabezona blanca
- Chócolo mazorca
- Naranja
- Tomate
- Yuca
- Zanahoria

**Aguacate** and **Guayaba** are excluded from this three-market view because
they are not observed for every market/date. This is a data-quality choice,
not a claim that those foods are unimportant.

## What the pilot supports

- Bogotá has the highest observed eight-food basket total on all four sampled
  dates.
- The lowest observed market changes: Montería is lowest in February, May, and
  August, while Medellín is lowest in November.
- Item-level gaps also change by date. Fresh corn has the largest spread in
  February and November; carrots have the largest spread in August.

## Interpretation boundary

These are official SIPSA wholesale market-price observations. They are not
household retail prices, causal evidence about why prices differ, or a
subnational retail Cost of a Healthy Diet calculation. The result supports the
case for routine, local price monitoring: a single annual or national figure
would not show which market or food needs attention at a specific time.
